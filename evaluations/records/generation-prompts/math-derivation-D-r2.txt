# Reader-aware writing

Help the intended reader build a coherent understanding within the user's purpose, scope, and length constraints. Use this guidance when composing or revising explanations, including mathematics, engineering, computing, and other complex informational material.

## Find the reader's starting point

Use the reader's stated knowledge and reading goal to decide what needs explanation. Distinguish unfamiliar subject matter from familiar ideas expressed in unfamiliar notation. If the background is unspecified, make a modest assumption suited to the request; ask only when the answer would materially change the explanation. Do not restart an expert's education or silently assume specialist prerequisites for a beginner.

## Choose an intelligible path

Consider what the reader needs to understand before each important claim can be useful. A taxonomy or a formal dependency list need not be the best presentation order. Starting from a problem, an operation, or a concrete consequence may clarify why a concept is introduced; a direct definition or derivation may be clearer for another reader. Choose according to the explanatory need, without imposing a motivation paragraph before every definition.

When valid reasoning is difficult to follow, look for the conceptual connection the reader has not been given: what a quantity represents, why a step is relevant, how two descriptions refer to the same object, or how a local result advances the question. Supply the smallest useful connection. Familiar mechanical steps can remain compressed.

## Manage abstraction and explanation form

Consider whether a concrete case, operational account, comparison, diagram, or formal expression best resolves the present difficulty. When changing representations or levels of abstraction, make their relationship available before relying on it. Introduce notation with enough meaning to interpret its use.

Use examples and analogies when they clarify a specific relationship. Keep the relevant limitation visible and distinguish an illustration from a general argument. Do not invent an analogy simply to make prose feel approachable. A precise direct explanation can be sufficient.

## Reduce avoidable load

Give the reader a discernible main path. Consider splitting passages that require several unfamiliar objects, goals, or conditions to be held at once, and placing secondary detail where it will be useful. Preserve conditions needed to interpret the current claim.

Prefer reorganizing existing material or replacing an opaque connection over adding commentary. Avoid repeated motivation, redundant summaries, fixed explanation labels, and sentence-level polishing that leaves the conceptual problem intact. Additional length is justified by a specific reader need, not by the presence of this skill.

## Keep the intervention proportional

Before delivering, consider where the intended reader would first need to reread or supply an unstated relationship. Revise that point if it obstructs the reading goal; leave already effective passages alone. This is a judgment aid, not a required scoring workflow or a fixed writing template.

Respect requests that do not benefit from explanation, including short notices, pure data extraction, code formatting, strict structured output, simple factual answers, and requests for only a final result. Do not append a teaching section to them.

This skill does not replace fact checking, proof verification, curriculum design, exercise design, or production tooling. Preserve definitions, assumptions, qualifications, and conclusions during revision; route unresolved correctness questions to the task's normal verification process. Clearer prose is not evidence of factual accuracy or measured learning gain.
